Tropics Scorecard · Singapore · Feb 2024 to Oct 2026

How close was the forecast to what the instruments measured?

I scored hourly rain, temperature and wind forecasts against Singapore's weather stations, not against a model's own analysis. Every comparison carries a 95 percent interval.

Open the full dashboard for every lead time, hour, season and station, or read the code.

01 · Rain

The best forecast beats the free baselines, but only just.

ECMWF's forecast is closer to the gauges than "what usually happens at this hour, in this month" at every lead time out to seven days. GFS, one day ahead, is not detectably better than assuming tomorrow repeats today.

A forecast can be real and still be a small step up from something free.

02 · Temperature

No model beats climatology at a weather station.

Both models run cool. ECMWF is at its worst between 9 in the morning and 3 in the afternoon. On its grid, the cell that holds most of Singapore is mostly sea, and sea does not heat up during the day the way a city does.

Mean error (forecast minus station) by local hour, one day ahead, in °C. Zero would be no bias. Shaded: 95 percent interval. Climatology sits 0.3 °C low because the test period was warmer than 2017 to 2023.

03 · Wind

Wind at a station is forecast worse than a guess from history.

Every model is further from the measured wind than climatology, and every model reads too windy. A grid cell 13 to 28 km wide, part of it open water, is being compared with a mast between buildings. They are not measuring the same thing, and anyone who needs the wind at that mast should know it.

Difference in mean absolute error against climatology on the same cases, one day ahead. Left of zero is better than climatology, right is worse. Bars are 95 percent intervals; an interval that crosses zero is no detectable difference.

04 · Matching

Which grid cell you pick matters as much as which model you pick.

A station has to be paired with one cell of the model's grid. Take the cell it sits in, or the nearest cell the model treats as land? For ECMWF the land rule moves 53 of Singapore's 54 southern stations to a cell centred in Johor.

I chose the cell the station sits in before I saw any score, and report the other as a check.

05 · Checking the data

Two of my first results were faults in the archive, not skill.

On the first run, GFS rain looked better seven days ahead than one day ahead. That cannot be right, so I looked. Beyond 120 hours the archived GFS rain is a third of its day-one amount. Forecasting a third of the rain lowers the error. I removed those lead times and wrote down why, even though it deleted a result that looked good.

The second fault runs the other way: ECMWF's public rain comes in 3-hour and 6-hour steps, so I added the 6-hour test in finding 1.

Mean forecast rain per hour over Singapore, by how far ahead the forecast was made. A model's average rain should not depend on lead time. ECMWF's holds steady. GFS drops by two thirds after day 3.

06 · Three events, replayed

Where the forecasts held, and where they did not.

Chosen from the gauge data and public reports before any forecast was looked at. The darkest line is what the gauges measured, as a network mean per hour. Colour is what each model said one day ahead.

What I am not claiming
  • A rain gauge is a point and a forecast is an average over a grid cell 9 to 28 km wide. Part of every low score is that mismatch.
  • The test is under three years, so each monsoon season appears two or three times.
  • The forecasts come from a public archive, and two faults were found in it. There may be others.
  • This is one region and one set of archives. It is not a ranking of models.

Method: the rules were written down before any forecast was scored. Baselines are persistence (the observation 24 hours earlier) and climatology (station, month and hour, 2017 to 2023). Intervals: moving-block bootstrap over days, 8-day blocks, 2000 resamples, on paired differences.
