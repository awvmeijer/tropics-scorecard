# Northeast monsoon surge, 19 to 20 March 2025

Status: draft, 13 November 2026. The numbers come from the replay; the diagnosis is a draft for the author to check against the soundings and the public reports, and to put in his own words.

Window: 18 March 12:00 to 21 March 12:00, Singapore time. Replay: `python -m tropics_scorecard.cases.replay surge_2025_03`.

## What happened

A surge of north-easterly winds over the South China Sea brought two days of near-continuous rain. The Meteorological Service Singapore recorded 272.3 mm over 19 and 20 March and the wettest March on record ([NEA](https://www.nea.gov.sg/media/news/news/index/spore-records-wettest-ever-march-and-hottest-ever-june-and-november-in-2025), [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2025/03/20/heavy-thundery-showers-across-singapore-on-march-20-morning-amid-monsoon-surge)).

From the gauges: 268.4 mm network mean over the window. The wettest hour for the network ended 10:00 on 20 March, at 20.6 mm.

## What each forecast said

Network-mean rain over the window, on the same stations and hours as the gauges.

| Source | Lead day 1 | Lead day 3 | Lead day 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gauges | 268.4 mm | | |
| GFS | 207.0 mm, peak hour 14.2 mm | 296.9 mm, peak 19.9 mm | 168.7 mm (archive fault at this lead, not scored) |
| ECMWF IFS 0.25 | 121.2 mm, peak 3.8 mm | 190.7 mm, peak 8.9 mm | 131.8 mm, peak 8.1 mm |
| Persistence | 222.4 mm | 4.0 mm | 5.6 mm |
| Climatology | 23.9 mm | | |

Both models saw a very wet spell three to five days ahead, five to twelve times the climatological amount. GFS came within about a quarter of the total at days 1 and 3. ECMWF IFS 0.25 forecast less than half the total at day 1, and less at day 1 than at day 3.

## The sounding

Changi, 18 and 19 March, 00 UTC: north-north-easterly winds of 9 to 12 metres per second at 925 and 850 hPa, and air close to saturation at 850 and 500 hPa by 19 March (dew point within 1 to 3 degrees of the temperature). A deep, moist, strong north-easterly flow: the signature of a surge.

## Diagnosis (draft)

The event was large in scale and driven by the monsoon flow, which global models carry well, so both gave days of warning. The amounts differ. The ECMWF cell that holds most of the island is mostly sea in the model, and its rain is spread over 3-hour steps in the archive, which caps the hourly peak (3.8 mm against 20.6 observed). The total is a fairer test, and there IFS was still too dry by half at day 1. To check: whether the model placed the rain band north or south of the island.

## Failure mode

Large-scale surge rain: timing forecast days ahead, amount under-forecast at a point, most by the coarser grid. Entered in `docs/failure_modes.md`.
