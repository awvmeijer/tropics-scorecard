# Case-study candidates

Status: chosen 13 November 2026: the March 2025 surge, the 17 September 2024 squall and the July 2024 dry spell. Chosen for the size and range of the events (record rain, the fastest convection, a long false-alarm test), before any forecast was looked at.

Shortlist written 11 November 2026. Two or three of these become case studies. Chosen from the gauge data first (`scripts/find_case_candidates.py`, which looks at no forecast), then checked against public reports that fix the dates. No forecast has been looked at for any of them.

| Candidate | Dates (Singapore) | What the gauges show | Public report | Why it is a useful test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Northeast monsoon surge | 19 to 20 March 2025 | Network mean 176.7 mm on 20 March, the wettest day in the test period; wettest gauge 209.6 mm. Network mean over the two days about 173 mm | Meteorological Service Singapore: wettest March on record, 272.3 mm over 19 to 20 March, 216.8 mm at Kallang on 20 March ([NEA](https://www.nea.gov.sg/media/news/news/index/spore-records-wettest-ever-march-and-hottest-ever-june-and-november-in-2025), [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2025/03/20/heavy-thundery-showers-across-singapore-on-march-20-morning-amid-monsoon-surge)) | A forecast warned of the surge days ahead. Did the models get the amount, or only the timing? |
| Northeast monsoon surge | 10 to 13 January 2025 | Three of the ten wettest days: 110.5 mm (10 Jan), 94.5 mm (11 Jan), 77.8 mm (13 Jan) network mean | Heavy rain warning 10 to 13 January; 255.2 mm at Changi over 10 and 11 January ([The Star, citing PUB](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2025/01/12/prolonged-rainfall-on-jan-10-and-11-exceeded-singapores-monthly-average-pub), [Mothership](https://mothership.sg/2025/01/january-monsoon-surge)) | A long event: tests whether skill holds over several days of the same regime |
| Sumatra squall | 17 September 2024, about 19:00 to 20:30 | In the hour ending 20:00, 60 of 62 gauges wet and 31 at 10 mm or more; by 21:00 it was over | An intense squall line swept across Singapore from 7 pm to 8:30 pm ([Ministry of Sustainability and the Environment, written reply](https://www.mse.gov.sg/latest-news/written-reply-to-pq-on-extreme-weather-conditions/), [Mothership](https://mothership.sg/2025/03/2024-singapore-weather-events/)) | Fast, organised convection at an unusual hour: the hardest case for an hourly forecast |
| Dry spell | 13 to 30 July 2024 | 18 days with network-mean rain under 1 mm a day, 1.6 mm in total; the longest dry run in the test period | Singapore's first dry spell since 2019, 13 to 30 July 2024 ([Mothership](https://mothership.sg/2025/03/2024-singapore-weather-events/), [NEA advisory](https://www.nea.gov.sg/media/news/advisories/index/less-wet-weather-expected-in-the-second-half-of-july-2024)) | Tests false alarms: does any forecast keep predicting showers that never came? |

The dry spell found in the gauge data matches the official dates to the day, which is a useful check that the reference table and its daily aggregation are right.

Other dates the data flags, not yet checked against reports: widespread intense hours at 04:00 on 22 June 2024, 14 September 2024 and 11 May 2025 (pre-dawn, the usual time for a Sumatra squall), and 15:00 on 13 April 2025 (43 of 56 gauges at 20 mm or more in one hour).
